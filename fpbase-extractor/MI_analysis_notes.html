2. Method recap

  • Representation. Sequences → length-normalized k=2 amino-acid k-mer frequency vectors (400-dim) → standardize → PCA. Spectra → resampled onto a common 350–750 nm grid → PCA.
  • Dimensionality. Everything reduced to 10 PCs. Sequence k-mers are information-spread (10 PCs ≈ 45% variance); spectra are smooth/redundant (10 PCs ≈ 96%).
  • Estimator. Kraskov–Stögbauer–Grassberger (KSG, 2004) algorithm 1, implemented from scratch with a cKDTree max-norm kNN search. Validated against the analytic Gaussian MI and against sklearn on the 1-D case.
  • Significance. Every reported MI is paired with a shuffled-pairing permutation null using the same estimator, at the same dimension and sample size. We report net MI (estimate − null mean), a z-score, and a permutation p-value.

4. The reliability question (the crux of the discussion)

The benchmark stress-tested ksg_mi on Gaussians of growing dimension with a known true MI (theory = −(d/2)·ln(1−ρ²) nats). The estimator is accurate in low dimension but increasingly under-reports as dimension grows:

10.2230.212−5%
51.1160.760−32%
102.2310.904−60%
153.3470.873−74%
The honest read

In high dimension, relative to sample size, the estimator becomes unreliable for absolute magnitude. This is the curse of dimensionality — fundamental to all nonparametric MI estimators, not a bug in KSG. It is driven by the dimension-to-N ratio: KSG is provably consistent (bias → 0 as N → ∞), but the samples needed to fill space grow exponentially with dimension.

"Which one is physical reality?"

In the benchmark, the theory is reality by construction — the data were generated from a known distribution, so its MI is a defined quantity, not an opinion. ksg_mi is simply the finite-sample estimate falling short. That the gap is measurable is the source of trust, not the source of doubt: an instrument whose error you have characterized is more trustworthy than one you have not. On the real data there is no theory line — the true distribution is unknown, so no method (KSG or otherwise) yields the absolute physical MI from finite samples.

What therefore is and isn't trustworthy

Use of the estimatorTrustworthy?Why
Detection — is there dependence (vs. null)?YesUnder independence the estimator is ~unbiased; the null is calibrated at the same d, N.
Ranking — which pair carries more?YesThe bias is monotonic, so it compresses the scale without reordering.
Absolute magnitude (bits) in high-D pairsLower boundDownward bias; the 20-D embedding pairs (N≈430) are in a worse regime than the d=10 benchmark row.

Practical consequence: the bar/heatmap values are best read as net-of-null, comparative quantities. The 20-D pairs (sequence↔spectrum, spectrum↔spectrum) are conservative lower bounds — the true dependence is likely higher than shown, which strengthens (not weakens) the qualitative conclusion that the dependence is real and large.

On "KSG is data-efficient"

No contradiction — different baselines. "Data-efficient" is comparative (vs. histogram/KDE estimators at the same task, mostly demonstrated in 1–2 dimensions). "Unreliable in high-D" is vs. ground truth, absolute. KSG degrades later and more gracefully than the alternatives; it does not escape the curse. Analogy: a fuel-efficient car still can't cross a continent on one tank.

5. Concepts clarified along the way

  • nats vs. bits. Same quantity, different log base. 1 nat = 1/ln2 ≈ 1.443 bits. KSG returns nats natively (digamma/natural logs); we convert to bits for reporting via NATS_TO_BITS = 1/np.log(2).
  • Interpreting MI magnitude. For continuous variables MI is unbounded above (no fixed scale), with 0 = independence. A useful intuition pump: under joint Gaussianity, ρ² = 1 − 2^(−2·MI_bits), so 1 bit ≈ 75% "shared variance", 2 bits ≈ 94%. Treat as intuition, not literal ρ.
  • z-score here. Standardized distance of the observed MI above its permutation null: (MI − null.mean) / null.std. A signal-to-noise / detectability measure, not a Gaussian p-value, and distinct from magnitude (a tight null can give high z at modest MI).
  • Custom ksg_mi vs. sklearn. sklearn's mutual_info_regression computes per-feature MI against a 1-D target — it cannot do joint multivariate X, nor multivariate-Y↔multivariate (sequence↔spectrum). The custom estimator was needed for those; sklearn was kept only as a 1-D validation cross-check.

6. Pinned & open

📌 Pinned

Estimator reliability in the high-dimensional pairs. The absolute MI for the 20-D pairs (sequence↔spectrum, spectrum↔spectrum) is a lower bound of unknown tightness. We paused here rather than resolving it. Detection and ranking conclusions stand; magnitude does not.

7. Potential next steps

Recommended first

Calibrate at the real operating points. Re-run the Gaussian benchmark at the exact (dx, dy, N) of each real feature pair, so the expected under-estimation is known and bounded there (rather than read off the pathological d=15 case). Annotate which heatmap cells are quantitatively reliable vs. lower-bound, and optionally apply a bias correction to the embedding pairs.

Cross-check with estimators that have different failure modes

The strongest evidence of trust is agreement across methods that fail differently — especially on ranking.

MethodEffortWhy it fits this data (10-D embeddings, N≈430)
Gaussian-copula MI (GCMI; Ince 2017)lowFast, robust at modest N; rank-transform + closed-form Gaussian MI. Ideal cross-check; flags KSG under-reporting.
LNC-corrected KSG (Gao 2015)lowNear drop-in; directly targets KSG's high-dependence underestimation.
Sliced MI (Goldfeld 2021)mediumAverages 1-D-projection MIs; scalable, stable in higher D (estimates a related quantity).
Neural (InfoNCE / MINE / SMILE)highScales to high-D, but data-hungry — likely worse than the above at N≈430; revisit only with much more data.

Other directions noted

  • Reduce dimension for the embedding↔embedding pairs (e.g. 5 PCs) and/or increase N — both directly shrink the bias gap.
  • Apples-to-apples comparison: restrict every pair to the common set of proteins for strict cross-representation comparability.
  • Richer sequence representation: 3-mers or learned protein-language-model embeddings (vs. k=2 k-mers) to capture finer structure — at the cost of more dimensions.
  • Reference: Czyż et al., "Beyond Normal: On the Evaluation of Mutual Information Estimators," NeurIPS 2023 — benchmark showing no estimator is uniformly best at high MI / high dimension. Useful sanity anchor.
